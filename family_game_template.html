How to spot the part that gives the sound
  1. Find the meaning part first. It's almost always one of a small set (below). In 91% of kanji that have a sound part, the other part is one of these.
  2. What's left is usually the sound, most often on the right (60%), sometimes on top, left or bottom.
  3. Check it: half the time the sound part is a kanji you already know, and its siblings share its reading: 青 せい → 晴 清 精 請 情 静, all せい.
  4. About a third of kanji have no usable sound part (it shifted on the way into Japanese); learn those readings through words.
